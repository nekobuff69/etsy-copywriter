## Essentials

-   Use all 13 tags and vary wording to avoid repetition.
-   Highlight what makes your product unique so shoppers can find it.
-   Keep each keyword phrase ≤20 characters; if a target search is longer, break it into multiple phrasal tags.
-   **Example**: <u>Targeting “minimalist diamond engagement rings” (a 35‑character phrase) → split into the three tags “minimalist jewelry”, “diamond ring”, and “engagement ring”. </u>
-   These phrasal chunks are more descriptive than single words like “minimalist” or “ring” while still covering all key terms shoppers might use.
-   Begin with clear, accurate descriptive tags, then try other tag types for broader reach.

## Other Inspirations

### Descriptive

The categories you add to your listings should describe what your product is, but adding a few descriptive tags lets you describe your product in your own words. Remember: Multi-word phrases are better than individual descriptive words.

**Examples:** 1920s cat brooch, reusable straw pouch, striped ceramic mug, set of four coasters

### Materials and techniques

-   Highlight product’s unique manufacturing details with tags
-   Include keywords for personalization/customization (e.g., “personalized,” “custom”)
-   Specify techniques or methods used (e.g., hammered cuff, custom embroidery)
-   Mention distinctive materials or features (e.g., reclaimed wood frame, personalized tumbler)

### Who it’s for

Give shoppers who are shopping for a gift but need inspiration some ideas with tags that describe who a product might be great for. Think of your target customer and who the ideal gift recipient might be.

**Examples:** Gifts for boyfriend, gifts for newlyweds, gifts for new moms, teacher gift

### Shopping occasions

-   Adding occasion‑based tags helps reach buyers looking for items suitable for a particular celebration. For example, think like a shopper searching for milestone‑celebration gifts or a host wanting to enhance an event.

-   Identify the phrases shoppers might use that align with your products (e.g., “birthday gift ideas,” “wedding party favors”).
-   Use occasion attributes (like “Christmas”) to label listings that fit those events, even if not specifically designed for them.

**Examples:** First anniversary, christening gifts, stocking stuffers, bachelorette party, animal themed party

### Solution-oriented

Maybe your products solve some need for a shopper, making their life a little easier or helping them feel like an awesome gift-giver/parent/friend. Imagine how a shopper looking for a solution to their problem (be it a blank wall in their apartment or a messy purse) might search for it.

**Examples:** Closet organization, workout headbands, lunch box decal, indoor garden

### Style

- Use descriptive tags that capture the unique style of each product to help shoppers find what they’re looking for.  
- Combine elements such as time period, color palette, or overall aesthetic with a word describing the item to create multi‑word, phrasal tags.

**Examples:** Art deco lamp, minimalist ring, rustic wall decor, typographic print

### Size
For some products, scale can be key. Try adding tags that describe the size and shape of your products to reach shoppers looking for the right fit.

**Examples:** Shallow basket, large beach bag, toddler pants, tiny gold hoops

### Tools for updating your titles and tags
  
- Quickly add or remove tags from multiple listings by selecting them in Shop Manager, choosing “Editing” → “Edit tags.”
- Use the quick edit tool to update titles and tags without opening each listing individually.
- Etsy offers an AI‑powered title suggestion tool that generates new titles based on your current title, first photo, and description.
- Title suggestions appear in the search visibility dashboard on Etsy.com or within the Seller app for eligible English listings.
